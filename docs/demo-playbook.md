# Demo and field playbook

## The two-to-four-minute demonstration

Use real completed work for the long-running evidence, then one small live action. Verify the final allotted demo duration with organizers.

**0:00–0:25 — state the business.** “We built Formshop, an agent-run custom-object shop. Founders give it a logo and a job to do; it designs a useful object and manages the order.” Hold up a physical sample only if one actually exists, or show a clearly identified digital deliverable.

**0:25–1:10 — show a real customer's request and result.** Display the original brief, timestamp, actual preview, dimensions, and downloadable exports. Explain what the customer bought. Redact private contact details. Do not use a sample logo as proof of a customer.

**1:10–1:55 — show the agents making a business decision.** Example: the requested width exceeded the supported range; the agent explained the limit and proposed a smaller design. Or quantity changed cost, so the company recalculated the quote and required a new approval. Show the resulting version and event evidence.

**1:55–2:40 — show the operation.** Display authentic approval, payment mode/status, delivery package or actual supplier acknowledgment, cost ledger, and founder interventions. A prepared RFQ does not count as a placed order. If only the digital service worked, say precisely that.

**2:40–3:15 — perform one live change.** Make a bounded request or revision; show the persisted run starting and real tool events. Do not stake the entire demo on a first-time long CAD build. Keep a labeled recording of the completed workflow in case venue internet fails.

**3:15–3:45 — explain the next business test.** Share measured customer outcomes, what was automated, the remaining manual boundary, and the next narrow expansion. Do not claim an evidence-free market size or guaranteed scale.

## Pitch language at three lengths

**One line:** “An agent-run shop that turns a founder's brief into a custom branded object and runs the order.”

**Thirty seconds:** “Custom physical products still require a person to coordinate design, revisions, quotes and production. We started with useful branded desk objects for startups. Our Naïve agents take the brief, build dimensioned CAD, check the result, prepare a scoped quote and manage approvals and delivery. The demo shows the actual files and business decisions, plus every place we had to intervene.”

**Expansion:** “The first catalog is deliberately small. If this works, we use the same specification and supplier history to handle repeat fixtures, displays and noncritical parts. The goal is a company that owns the job, with quality and margin controls—not a founder manually shepherding every generated model.”

Only change present-tense capability claims to “built/does” after implementation proves them. This document is a proposed script, not evidence those capabilities exist now.

## Live operating scorecard

Track: opt-in briefs; eligible jobs; QA-passed artifacts; customer approvals; delivered packages; confirmed physical orders; real revenue excluding tax/refunds; actual inference/tool spend; supplier costs; revision count; and founder interventions by stage.

For any autonomy percentage, define the denominator and sample. Example: “Of 12 completed required stage transitions across 2 delivered jobs, 9 completed without founder intervention.” Keep customer approvals separate. Also show failed/blocked jobs so a high percentage cannot hide discarded failures. Do not call revenue minus model cost profit.

## Customer conversations and offer

Use [the acquisition playbook](customer-acquisition.md) for channel priorities, $39 scope, first-use flow and ready-to-use drafts. Recruitment is founder-led initially; disclose this separately from agent-handled customer operations. Likes and free previews are not paid orders or downstream use.

In-person invitation the team may use: “We’re testing an agent-run shop for useful custom desk objects. Is there a real display or branded object your team needs? We can show you a dimensioned design and an exact scope before you commit.”

Ask for a specific job, budget, quantity and next deadline. Let the customer approve logo use and follow-up. Do not scrape attendees and launch unsolicited email. No outreach has been sent as part of this planning task.

## Useful optional mobile demonstration

For a QR display, a real phone scans the actual QR insert and reaches the approved customer URL. If using Mobilerun, record the actual device run, browser result and any failures. This is genuine product QA, but scanning a digital preview alone does not validate a physical printed QR. A supplier mobile-app automation is only worth adding if it is permitted, reliable, and removes a real fulfillment step.

## Questions judges may ask

- **What runs without you?** Show the actual stage ledger and interruptions; identify any human file export, supplier submission, customer recruitment or fabrication.
- **What did customers buy?** Name the deliverable and payment evidence. Distinguish an unpaid pilot from a sale.
- **Why not just use a CAD agent?** CAD is one production step; demonstrate qualification, QA, scoped revision, cost/approval and delivery.
- **Can it make arbitrary parts?** No current claim. Explain the supported family and why constrained quality checks matter.
- **Who manufactures it?** Name the real confirmed provider or say that only digital fulfillment was completed.
- **What happens on a failed print or supplier outage?** Show the policy, saved failure, cost reserve, and actual customer communication where available.
- **How does this scale?** Discuss repeat orders and proven templates; label broader expansion as a hypothesis.

## Before presenting

Reconcile every displayed count to persisted evidence. Open the actual artifact download. Verify test/live labels and exact design version. Confirm customer permission for public evidence. Make one clean backup recording, label it with its timestamp, and rehearse an honest failure case. Stop building at Sunday 11am.
