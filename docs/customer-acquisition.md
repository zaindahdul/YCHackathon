# Get the first real customers before judging

Decision date: Saturday, November 7, 2026, approximately 13:40 Pacific. About 21 hours remain before the Sunday 11am cutoff, including rest and verification. This is an executable marketing plan, not evidence that outreach has been sent or customers acquired. No messages or posts were sent as part of this work.

## The recommendation

**Use founder-led introductions to recruit a few real jobs; let the agent company handle the work and the agreed follow-up.** The initial market is people who need a particular physical object, not everyone who enjoys an AI-generated render. Sell a finished outcome with a price and scope. Keep recruitment time bounded so both founders can still build.

The weakest assumption in the earlier plan is that a founder wants to buy a CAD file for a QR display. Many will only value the physical object. A render can open a conversation, but it does not fix that mismatch. Without a confirmed printer, prioritize buyers who already have a fabrication route and explicitly want design help. Don't promise a display for tomorrow when all we can deliver is a file.

The operating goal is **one legitimate customer job delivered and used**, then a second independent job through the same path. It is more valuable than a large waitlist. Payment is stronger demand evidence than a free pilot; physical use, a fabrication quote, a file download and a paid design order must be reported separately.

## Who to approach first

| Prospect | Buying trigger | Qualifying question | Appropriate offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Startup with an upcoming booth/demo/customer event | A specific presentation/display deadline | “What are you putting on the table, and when do you need it?” | Supported branded QR display; digital package only if they have a printing route |
| Hardware/maker team with a real prototype and printer/provider | Needs a presentation cradle or simple noncritical fixture | “Do you already have a printer, measured dimensions and a specific part you need?” | Design service for a supported family; if cradle demand wins, replace the QR template once rather than add a second product immediately |
| Founder already planning team/customer gifts | An existing purchase, quantity and budget | “What were you going to order, and who approves it?” | A design/production proposal with physical price and date confirmed separately |

An attendee without a company, near-term need or buying authority may be a useful tester or introduction source. Do not count them as a commercial prospect merely because the event is at YC. YC companies are one audience; don't imply YC endorsement.

Ask three questions before expensive customization: **What is the job and deadline? Who can approve the purchase? How will it be fabricated?** If the answers are vague, show a generic sample and move on. A full custom job should not be a gift to every curious passerby.

Do not assume access or spend time creating a community. No cold-email blast, paid ads, SEO campaign or large launch-site preparation belongs on the immediate path. Those channels have uncertain time to a qualified response; we can learn faster by speaking to nearby buyers.

## The offer that gets someone to act

Use **“a custom object for your next demo”** as the hook. “An autonomous company” explains our implementation and is relevant to judges; the customer needs a useful result.

Once the pipeline works, show one real sample and offer:

> We’re testing a custom-object shop for startup teams. Do you need a branded desk display for an upcoming demo or event? We can show a design preview and agree the exact deliverable before you pay. The $39 pilot covers an approved, dimensioned design package and one revision. Printing and shipping are separate and require a confirmed quote.

$39 is one concrete price experiment inside the existing $29–$49 range, not a proven price or a new commitment to a physical item. Use this copy only when the package can actually be delivered within an agreed window. Before that, say “we’re interviewing teams about this” and collect a real brief without claiming the product is live.

If printer access becomes confirmed, offer a physical version with the actual unit price, quantity, pickup/shipping conditions and delivery date. Do not substitute the digital service without explaining the difference. If buyers only want a physical object and cannot print it, an unpaid future-production lead is a lead, not a validated design-file business.

Free preview policy: one quick, bounded preview for a **qualified** request, with a small daily capacity set by the measured generation cost and time. Editable/source files, QA and the included revision belong to the agreed paid package. Free pilots can be used for learning, but label their price and founder assistance honestly. No artificial discount, paid testimonial or fake scarcity. “Three pilot slots” is only valid if three is the actual support capacity.

## Reduce the first-use work

The first CTA is **“Show us what your team needs.”** Ask for company/URL, job/use date and a reply channel. Show the scope and price before the customer invests in a long CAD questionnaire. Then gather dimensions, quantity and asset authorization only for the selected job. Do not require an account before showing a sample.

The response should lead to one action at a time: confirm the brief, inspect the actual design, request a specific revision, approve a version and price, pay if enabled, then receive the package. Agree when the customer can review it so a late response does not stall the demo. The site/app is still unbuilt; these are flow requirements, not claims of a working link.

Don't promise “a design in five minutes” until repeated runs demonstrate that latency. Show a sample immediately and give a measured estimate for their own job. Cap the queue; a growing backlog the company cannot fulfill is poor marketing.

| 60–90 min | Ask for a specific revision, exact approval and $39 scoped order if ready | Run checks and package/export; save events | A substantive customer next step, not a compliment |

If the CAD path is still not working, do not sell a timed delivery. Continue interviews while Zain resolves it. These are work timeboxes, not guaranteed pipeline performance. If a physical provider is found, a founder confirms the real file, price and deadline before promoting physical delivery.

**Decision gate:** after 6–8 relevant conversations and up to 90 minutes, ask whether anyone has a current job, buying authority and willingness to take a meaningful next step. This is a practical stop rule, not statistical proof. If all responses are “cool” and none supply a real need, stop polishing the QR-stand pitch. First change the audience to people with printers/current events; if several prospects instead need the same simple cradle/fixture, explicitly replace the first template and update the scope. Do not grow an arbitrary-CAD backlog. If nobody values the design deliverable without a shipped object, solve fabrication access or acknowledge that the current offer has not found demand.

## Once there is a real result

At dinner, do a second short recruitment pass with the actual approved before/after and disclosed delivery scope. An introduction to one relevant team is a better ask than “share this everywhere.” Post one permitted example if a relevant group is available. Avoid turning two builders into a full-time marketing team.

Before the evening ends, get at least one customer to approve or reject a real version, and complete the agreed paid/free delivery. Schedule the review before people sleep. In the morning, verify what they actually did with it and resolve a meaningful revision; freeze marketing that would create unfulfillable jobs near the cutoff.

If nothing is delivered by the evening, stop acquiring more leads and fix the first job. A pipeline full of requests is not a running design shop.

## Ready-to-use copy — drafts only

**In person:** “Do you have an upcoming demo or event where you need a custom display? We’re building a shop that handles the design and order. If you have a real need, I can show you what’s supported.”

**Warm-introduction request:** “Do you know one founder with an upcoming demo, booth or small custom-part job—and ideally a printer or fabricator? We’re testing a scoped custom-object design service today. An introduction to someone with that actual need would help more than a broad repost.”

**Group post, after a real sample exists:** “Building at the Naïve hackathon: custom branded desk displays for startup demos. Here’s an actual design example. We’re taking a small number of pilot jobs for a $39 design package with one revision; printing/shipping are separately quoted. If your team has a real upcoming use, reply with what you need and when. We’ll confirm fit and delivery scope before payment.” Attach only a real, approved example. Don't add a signup link until one works.

**Opted-in follow-up:** “Your preview is ready. Does it meet the use and dimensions we agreed? Reply with the one change you need, or approve this version and the design-package scope. Printing is not included.” Adapt to the actual quoted scope. One agreed follow-up is enough; don't repeatedly ping busy hackers.

**Referral after delivery:** “What will you do with the files next? If the result was useful, is there one team with a similar upcoming need you’d introduce us to? May we show this design publicly?” No public logo/testimonial without consent.

## What agents can do for marketing

The founders bootstrap the first introductions. Count that as human acquisition; do not pitch it as autonomous prospect discovery. Agents can then qualify opted-in requests, gather missing details, produce a relevant preview, ask for a revision/approval, prepare a quote from a real rate card, deliver the package and draft a follow-up or case study. Record each actual action and whether a founder approved or repaired it.

A marketing agent can decide which **qualified, consented** lead to prioritize using the job, urgency, supported geometry and cost—not invent demand or take over unapproved communications. It can recommend a messaging change from recorded objections and propose a customer-approved gallery entry. Scheduled follow-up requires a configured communication policy and the customer's agreed channel/time. This planning request does not authorize sending any of these messages.

The first honest autonomous loop is **an opt-in request → agent clarification → generated offer → customer reply → revised/approved deliverable → feedback**. It can coexist with founder-led initial recruiting. If all sales and customer handling remain manual, report that boundary; don't hide it behind the word “autonomous.”

## The funnel and what counts

Track one row per independent prospect/job: source, buyer role, job/deadline, fabrication access, permission/channel, agreed scope/price, stage, next action/time, outcome and rejection reason. Add a simple source tag such as room/warm/group/referral; no analytics stack is needed.

Count separately: conversations → qualified briefs → previews viewed → substantive revisions/approvals → paid digital orders → complete digital deliveries → downstream use → acknowledged physical orders → actual physical deliveries. These are distinct events, not interchangeable conversion labels.

A reasonable learning goal is 6–8 conversations, 2–3 qualified briefs, one approved/used design and one genuine paid order if the offer and payment setup support it. These are targets, not forecasts; show the actual denominator and results even if zero. A file opened in a slicer or submitted for a fabrication quote demonstrates downstream activity; it does not prove fabrication or physical use. A free download or “looks cool” demonstrates curiosity only. Founder purchases, team purchases, sponsor credits and test payments are not independent customer revenue.
